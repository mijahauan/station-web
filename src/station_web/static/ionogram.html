Time of flight

Propagation delay of each tick: detection time minus scheduled broadcast time, on the station's registered sample clock. Clusters suggest distinct propagation modes (1F2, 2F2, 1E and so on).
Reading the charts: Rank-0 arrivals (dominant path) cluster near the model-predicted time of flight. Rank-1+ arrivals (secondary paths) appear at longer delays; a separate cluster suggests a distinct propagation mode (1F, 2F, sporadic-E, sidescatter). The delay between two modes depends on ground range and on both hop geometries, so this page derives no layer height from it. SNR helps separate modes: each extra hop crosses the D layer twice more and arrives weaker.

Doppler against delay: One point per minute and station. X: the minute's median time of flight of the dominant arrivals (ms). Y: that minute's carrier Doppler (Hz), from the phase page's estimator, fD = −(1/2π)·Σdφ/Σdt over the 1 Hz carrier phase. Modes that move differently could separate along the Doppler axis even where their delays overlap. On a SHARED channel the Doppler belongs to the composite carrier (WWV + WWVH + BPM), so every station's delay in a minute pairs with the same Doppler.
