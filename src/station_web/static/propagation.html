SNR against time
One colour per frequency (L2_timing_measurements). With one station selected, the dotted gold line gives the solar elevation at that path's midpoint: low frequencies fade when the sun lights the D layer, high frequencies need a sunlit F layer.
Arrival residual against the model (ms)
Each broadcast's tick arrival minus the modelled propagation delay. A diagnostic, not ionospheric science: these residuals feed the T3 estimate of t₀. Residuals within 0.5 ms of ±5, 10 or 15 ms sit on the tick lattice and suggest a lock on the wrong tick or mode; the chart draws them as grey crosses, marked suspect. Values railed at ±20 ms stay out. Doppler lives on the Ionosphere page.

Modes

Mode assigned to each measurement
hf-timestd names a propagation mode (1F2, 2F2, 1E and so on) for each measurement; one row per station and frequency.

MUF and mode shares, hour by hour

hamsci-physics's reanalysis writes these each hour (L3C_propagation_stats). It estimates each path's MUF as the highest frequency whose F-layer probability tops 0.5; an hour with too few F-layer frequencies gets no MUF.

Detections per broadcast

Attempts, detections and rejections
hf-timestd tries to measure every expected tick and records each attempt (L2_detection_attempts). Green counts the attempts that passed every gate; the other colours count rejections by reason, for example corr_snr_low (correlation SNR under the gate) or arrival_tolerance (the tick arrived outside its window). The server counts them; no row leaves the database.

Path map

Great-circle paths from the receiver
An azimuthal-equidistant map centred on the receiver: each path runs straight out from the centre along its bearing, and distance from the centre reads true. BPM's path crosses the Arctic, as the signal travels it. The receiver comes from /api/station/metadata, the transmitters from the broadcast registry.

Time of flight

Propagation delay of each tick: detection time minus scheduled broadcast time, on the station's registered sample clock. Clusters suggest distinct propagation modes.

Reading the charts: Rank-0 arrivals (dominant path) cluster near the model-predicted time of flight. Rank-1+ arrivals (secondary paths) appear at longer delays; a separate cluster suggests a distinct propagation mode (1F, 2F, sporadic-E, sidescatter). The delay between two modes depends on ground range and on both hop geometries, so this page derives no layer height from it. SNR helps separate modes: each extra hop crosses the D layer twice more and arrives weaker.

Doppler against delay: One point per minute and station. X: the minute's median time of flight of the dominant arrivals (ms). Y: that minute's carrier Doppler (Hz), from the Ionosphere page's estimator, fD = +(1/2π)·Σdφ/Σdt over the 1 Hz carrier phase. Modes that move differently could separate along the Doppler axis even where their delays overlap. On a SHARED channel the Doppler belongs to the composite carrier (WWV + WWVH + BPM), so every station's delay in a minute pairs with the same Doppler.
