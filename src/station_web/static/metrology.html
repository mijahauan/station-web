How the timing works

Each sample the receiver takes gets its own UTC instant. The station computes that instant from the sample's count, the rate the converter runs at, and one registration. A GPS-disciplined oscillator sets the rate, and we call it the ruler. The registration pins one sample on the ruler to a known UTC instant. Several estimators measure the registration, each by its own method, and the tiers rank them by uncertainty, T6 best. The station puts the best credible estimator in force and keeps the rest as witnesses. D_clock describes the host computer's clock: how far it sits from the registration. The station derives D_clock and hands it to chrony; no data product depends on it.

The upper panel draws the calibrated value as a median line with its sigma band; the lower panel draws the raw value. hf-timestd subtracts per-station calibration to get from raw to calibrated, and the facts above name that offset. Points that land 5, 10, 15 or 20 ms from a rolling median (more than 3 sigma away) look like a wrong tick lock, and points more than 5 sigma away look like outliers. The page marks both kinds apart and leaves them out of the median. The server draws at most 1500 points per line.

Host clock: D_clock and chrony
D_clock: host clock minus registration, the quantity chrony disciplines

chrony reports each source as the local clock minus that source. FUSE carries the T3 registration and HPPS the T6 one, so their lines read as host clock minus registration. A source recovered from the sample stream may not vote on the host clock (§7.1.1), so both appear as noselect witnesses. The grey band spans the NTP pool: its lowest, median and highest source.
